# Communication and shared language

Communicating intent is the skill that separates great engineers, and a precise shared vocabulary (with people or with agents) is how understanding gets established.
  > "my pet theory here and this goes to teaching as well is that once you understand and establish the language of something you understand it" — [youtube-appearances/unknown_d_BuVJgadxs_agent-skills-with-matt-pocock.md](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d_BuVJgadxs&t=965s) · Unhandled Exception Podcast

## Evidence

- He calls communication a superpower, not a soft skill, and credits his voice-coach years.
  > "it's not a soft skill, it's a superpower." — [youtube-appearances/unknown_kJD_1tb6-NE_imposter-syndrome-is-lying-to-you.md](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kJD_1tb6-NE&t=951s) · Visual Studio Code
  > "the ability to communicate always just felt like a ridiculous overpowered skill that I had in my locker that no one else had." — [youtube-appearances/unknown_rlM_fAKxB3Q_senior-dev-this-grill-me-prompt.md](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rlM_fAKxB3Q&t=647s) · Latent Space
- Great programmers communicate intent, especially in writing.
  > "the really good programmers are great communicators and especially in writing" — [youtube-appearances/unknown_x18rpm3zGEE_i-quit-voice-coaching-for-typescript.md](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=x18rpm3zGEE&t=309s) · Backend Banter
- Bad at AI usually means bad with people.
  > "If you're bad at working with AI, you're probably also bad at working with people" — [x/2025-11.md](https://x.com/mattpocockuk/status/1985326992640913701) · 2025-11
- A shared domain language with the agent changes results "night and day".
  > "if you get the agent to use the domain language the difference is night and day because suddenly you're speaking the same language." — [youtube-appearances/unknown_4DhcSPkEbwI_ai-skills-with-matt-pocock.md](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4DhcSPkEbwI&t=3465s) · The Pragmatic Engineer
  > "Domain language is unbelievably powerful with agents" — [x/2026-08.md](https://x.com/mattpocockuk/status/2083641584705253414) · 2026-08
- Consistency is the whole point.
  > "consistent language is the whole point, and a term nobody uses consistently is worse than no term." — [blog/skills-codebase-design.md](https://www.aihero.dev/skills-codebase-design) · 2026-07
- Naming is not bike-shedding; it shapes every generated line.
  > "This might just feel like bike-shedding to you. But, this is going to affect every part of the code that's generated." — [youtube/2026-05-14_6BB6exR8Zd8_i-stopped-using-grill-me-for.md](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6BB6exR8Zd8&t=717s) · 2026-05
- He hates jargon that makes simple things feel hard.
  > "There's something I hate more than anything in the world, and that is when people make something that is actually simple feel confusing for their benefit." — [youtube/2026-07-15_CE57AICfAew_a-dictionary-of-ai-coding.md](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/CE57AICfAew?t=0s) · 2026-07

## How it shows up

- Nearly every recent repo has a glossary so he and the agents use the same short words.
  > "Names the raw materials, containers, channels, activities, and disciplines of the work so that Matt and AI agents can speak the same language." — [github/mise-en-place/CONTEXT.md](https://github.com/mattpocock/mise-en-place/blob/HEAD/CONTEXT.md)
- He calls the ubiquitous-language technique possibly the coolest in his skills repo.
  > "It's hard to explain how powerful this is. It might be the single coolest technique in this repo." — [github/skills/README.md](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills/blob/HEAD/README.md)
- He steers agents with "leading words" mined from classic books.
  > "I just started diving into different books, all the books I could find to try to mine them for leading words." — [youtube-appearances/unknown_4DhcSPkEbwI_ai-skills-with-matt-pocock.md](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4DhcSPkEbwI&t=3333s) · The Pragmatic Engineer
  > "I've realised that all of the great skills I've written share one thing in common. They make heavy use of Leitwörter - leading words." — [x/2026-06.md](https://x.com/mattpocockuk/status/2066922013000671731) · 2026-06
- He wrote a plain-English dictionary of AI coding against "manufactured" confusion.
  > "A lot of the confusion is manufactured: **there's a whole VC-funded economy that benefits from keeping it hard to understand**." — [github/dictionary-of-ai-coding/README.md](https://github.com/mattpocock/dictionary-of-ai-coding/blob/HEAD/README.md)
- As a teacher he splits time between learning a topic and finding the phrases for it.
  > "I need to divide my time between learning the stuff and figuring out the right phrases to use about it." — [youtube-appearances/unknown_rlM_fAKxB3Q_senior-dev-this-grill-me-prompt.md](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rlM_fAKxB3Q&t=662s) · Latent Space
- He renames things when names are wrong (PRD to spec).
  > "The thing that we were creating in two PRD wasn't actually a PRD. It was a spec." — [youtube/2026-07-08_A8mokin_YOs_new-skills-v1-1-brings-wayfinder.md](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A8mokin_YOs&t=63s) · 2026-07

## Tensions and evolution

- He moved from DDD vocabulary to plainer names: `context.md` became `GLOSSARY.md` in 2026.
  > "I still use DDD in all but name, but I wanted a slightly less overloaded term here." — [youtube/2026-10-05_BsJGo1wFTvQ_new-skills-v1-3-brings-pr.md](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BsJGo1wFTvQ&t=530s) · 2026-10
  > "Over time CONTEXT.md has shrunk to literally just a glossary, so this makes sense." — [x/2026-08.md](https://x.com/mattpocockuk/status/2088722635999834182) · 2026-08
- His own register went from hype in early READMEs to deliberately de-hyped prose.
  > "**Enter Boilersuit**, the blazingly-fast, bug-proof way of working with Redux in react-boilerplate." — [github/boilersuit/README.md](https://github.com/mattpocock/boilersuit/blob/HEAD/README.md)
  > "Write in a plain, de-hyped register. No selling the concept" — [github/dictionary-of-ai-coding/CLAUDE.md](https://github.com/mattpocock/dictionary-of-ai-coding/blob/HEAD/CLAUDE.md)
