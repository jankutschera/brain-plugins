# Ramps to knowledge

He builds ramps: each step depends only on the one before, and complexity grows one layer at a time, often in historical order.
  > "In my mind, education is the very difficult technical process of building ramps to knowledge." — [youtube-appearances/unknown_lXUZvyajciY_andrej-karpathy-we-re-summoning-ghosts.md](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lXUZvyajciY&t=7401s) · 2025-10

## Evidence

- Building the ramp is a second, separate job after building the thing, and most technical people skip it.
  > "TLDR: Step 1 build the thing. Step 2 build the ramp." — [x/2024-02.md](https://x.com/karpathy/status/1760388761349927356) · 2024-02
  > "you might be leaving somewhere 10-100X of the potential of that exact same piece of work on the table just because you haven't made it sufficiently accessible." — [x/2024-02.md](https://x.com/karpathy/status/1760388761349927356) · 2024-02
  > "I would say there's a lot of people building a thing I would say there's lot a lot less happening of like building ramps so that people can actually understand all this stuff" — [youtube-appearances/unknown_c3b-JASoPi0_making-ai-accessible-with-andrej-karpathy.md](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c3b-JASoPi0&t=1959s) · 2024-03
- The core task is untangling knowledge so each step rests only on earlier ones.
  > "I feel like education is the most intellectually interesting thing because you have a tangle of understanding and you're trying to lay it out in a way that creates a ramp where everything only depends on the thing before it." — [youtube-appearances/unknown_lXUZvyajciY_andrej-karpathy-we-re-summoning-ghosts.md](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lXUZvyajciY&t=8346s) · 2025-10
- He measures a ramp by understanding per second and by never leaving the learner stuck.
  > "It's giving you a lot of what I call eurekas per second, which is understanding per second. That's what I want, lots of eurekas per second." — [youtube-appearances/unknown_lXUZvyajciY_andrej-karpathy-we-re-summoning-ghosts.md](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lXUZvyajciY&t=7423s) · 2025-10
  > "I want to figure out how to build these ramps very efficiently so that people are never stuck and everything is always not too hard or not too trivial, and you have just the right material to progress." — [youtube-appearances/unknown_lXUZvyajciY_andrej-karpathy-we-re-summoning-ghosts.md](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lXUZvyajciY&t=7443s) · 2025-10
- Zero to Hero starts at a bigram model and complexifies it, lecture by lecture, into a GPT.
  > "We implement a bigram character-level language model, which we will further complexify in followup videos into a modern Transformer language model, like GPT." — [github/nn-zero-to-hero/README.md](https://github.com/karpathy/nn-zero-to-hero/blob/73c3fcc741f0ec104ca850b1fb0df90e7e8d4cde/README.md) · 2024-08
  > "Future lectures will gradually complexify the neural net to take more than one input character" — [x/2022-09.md](https://x.com/karpathy/status/1567592848165601281) · 2022-09
  > "So, very simple bigram and bag of word models, multi-layer perceptrons, recurrent neural networks, all the way to modern transformers." — [youtube/2022-10-09_PaCmpygFfXo_the-spelled-out-intro-to-language.md](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PaCmpygFfXo&t=130s) · 2022-09
- Inside one artifact he adds one component at a time, like layers of an onion.
  > "To see the code built up piece by piece as layers of the onion" — [blog/karpathy.github.io/2026__02__12__microgpt.md](https://karpathy.github.io/2026/02/12/microgpt/) · 2026-02
  > "I think this might be one helpful way to step through the code base, where you add one component at a time." — [blog/karpathy.github.io/2026__02__12__microgpt.md](https://karpathy.github.io/2026/02/12/microgpt/) · 2026-02
- He opens with history, so the learner sees why a thing exists.
  > "OK, so I wanted to provide a little bit of context on why does this transformers class even exist. So a little bit of historical context." — [youtube-appearances/unknown_XfpMkf4rD6E_stanford-cs25-v2-i-introduction-to.md](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XfpMkf4rD6E&t=639s) · 2023-01
  > "this is going to be very important to understand the history of the development of these architectures" — [youtube/2022-10-09_P6sfmUTpUmc_building-makemore-part-3-activations-gradients.md](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P6sfmUTpUmc&t=41s) · 2022-10
- His projects form a lineage, and each one points back to the earlier lesson.
  > "If you don't know too much about autograd, I'd encourage you to watch my" — [github/llm.c/doc/layernorm/layernorm.md](https://github.com/karpathy/llm.c/blob/f1e2ace651495b74ae22d45d1723443fd00ecd3a/doc/layernorm/layernorm.md) · 2024-04
  > "remember from our microG grad video earlier addition distributes gradients equally to both of its branches" — [youtube/2023-10-09_kCc8FmEb1nY_let-s-build-gpt-from-scratch.md](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kCc8FmEb1nY&t=5367s) · 2023-01
- He motivates a dry topic before the hard part, by tying it to failures people know.
  > "I just want to make sure that we motivate it sufficiently for why we are doing all this and why this is so gross" — [youtube/2024-10-09_zduSFxRajkE_let-s-build-the-gpt-tokenizer.md](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zduSFxRajkE&t=264s) · 2024-02
  > "a lot of the shortcomings of LLMs that may be attributed to the neural network or otherwise appear mysterious actually trace back to tokenization." — [github/minbpe/lecture.md](https://github.com/karpathy/minbpe/blob/1acefe89412b20245db5a22d2a02001e547dc602/lecture.md) · 2024-02

## How it shows up

- He states the entry point and tells beginners to go in order.
  > "Assumes basic knowledge of Python and a vague recollection of calculus from high school." — [github/nn-zero-to-hero/README.md](https://github.com/karpathy/nn-zero-to-hero/blob/73c3fcc741f0ec104ca850b1fb0df90e7e8d4cde/README.md) · 2024-08
  > "If you’re a beginner I would watch the playlist from start and in order" — [x/2024-06.md](https://x.com/karpathy/status/1799972032622493910) · 2024-06
- He orders topics from easy to hard and says why.
  > "training is significantly more complex and involved, both algorithmically and computationally, and that's partly why I started by writing inference (llama2.c) before I implemented training (llm.c, here)." — [github/llm.c/doc/layernorm/layernorm.md](https://github.com/karpathy/llm.c/blob/f1e2ace651495b74ae22d45d1723443fd00ecd3a/doc/layernorm/layernorm.md) · 2024-04
- In talks he names the parts up front.
  > "I would like to partition the talk into two parts. In the first part, I would like to tell you about how we train GPT Assistance, and then in the second part, we're going to take a look at how we can use these assistants effectively for your applications." — [youtube-appearances/unknown_bZQun8Y4L2A_state-of-gpt-brk216hfs.md](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bZQun8Y4L2A&t=31s) · 2023-05
  > "first I'm going to give you a short primer on neural networks and how they work and how they're trained and I need to do this because I need to explain in the second part why it is such a big deal that we have the fleet" — [youtube-appearances/unknown_Ucp0TTmvqOE_tesla-autonomy-day.md](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ucp0TTmvqOE&t=6839s) · 2019-04
- He guards the audience's limited bandwidth.
  > "your audience has a fixed bit bandwidth" — [blog/karpathy.github.io/2016__09__07__phd.md](https://karpathy.github.io/2016/09/07/phd/) · 2016-09
  > "A common failure case (as an audience member) is to see talks where I’m painfully bored during the first half and completely confused during the second half, learning nothing by the end." — [blog/karpathy.github.io/2016__09__07__phd.md](https://karpathy.github.io/2016/09/07/phd/) · 2016-09
- He looks for the best existing explanation of each concept.
  > "a phrase I first heard in a chat with @3blue1brown a while ago but has lingered with me since. Why shouldn’t there be an effort to seek/recognize such a thing for all concepts" — [x/2018-09.md](https://x.com/karpathy/status/1038476966997852160) · 2018-09
- He insists the ramp is necessary, against his own engineer's instinct.
  > "Some voice in your head will tell you that this is not necessary, but it is wrong." — [x/2024-02.md](https://x.com/karpathy/status/1760388761349927356) · 2024-02

## Tensions and evolution

- In 2016 he wrote that he despises the outline slide; in 2021 and 2023 talks he still announces the structure aloud.
  > "For instance, I despise the outline slide." — [blog/karpathy.github.io/2016__09__07__phd.md](https://karpathy.github.io/2016/09/07/phd/) · 2016-09
  > "i'm going to roughly organize this section chronologically so starting off with some of the neural networks and what they looked like roughly four years ago when i joined the team and how they have developed over time" — [youtube-appearances/unknown_j0z4FweCy4M_tesla-ai-day-2021.md](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j0z4FweCy4M&t=3079s) · 2021-08
- In 2026 he moved the ramp-walking to agents: he explains to the agent and the agent adapts it to each person.
  > "If you can explain it to agents, then agents can be the router and they can actually target it to the human in their language uh with infinite uh you know, patience" — [youtube-appearances/unknown_kwSVtQ7dziU_skill-issue-andrej-karpathy-on-code.md](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kwSVtQ7dziU&t=3793s) · 2026-03
