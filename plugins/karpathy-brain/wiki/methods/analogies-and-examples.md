# Analogies and examples

He explains with analogies, coined phrases and concrete, often personal examples.
  > "they let you pull in structure laterally from one domain to another as as a way of generating entropy and reaching unique thoughts." — [x/2025-10.md](https://x.com/karpathy/status/1973756330449236009) · 2025-10

## Evidence

- Physical images stand in for the math: circuits, forces, springs.
  > "In my opinion, the best way to think of Neural Networks is as real-valued circuits" — [blog/karpathy.github.io/neuralnets.md](https://karpathy.github.io/neuralnets/) · n.d.
  > "The derivative can be thought of as a force on each input as we pull on the output to become higher." — [blog/karpathy.github.io/neuralnets.md](https://karpathy.github.io/neuralnets/) · n.d.
  > "And so you can think of this as adding like a spring force or like a gravity force that that pushes W to be zero." — [youtube/2022-10-09_PaCmpygFfXo_the-spelled-out-intro-to-language.md](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PaCmpygFfXo&t=6810s) · 2022-09
- He has used the same car-and-bicycle picture for the chain rule in a 2022 video and a 2026 post.
  > "if a car travels twice as fast as bicycle and the bicycle is four times as fast as walking man then the car travels two times four eight times as fast as demand" — [youtube/2022-10-09_VMj-3S1tku0_the-spelled-out-intro-to-neural.md](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VMj-3S1tku0&t=2594s) · 2022-08
  > "If a car travels twice as fast as a bicycle and the bicycle is four times as fast as a walking man, then the car travels 2 x 4 = 8 times as fast as the man." — [blog/karpathy.github.io/2026__02__12__microgpt.md](https://karpathy.github.io/2026/02/12/microgpt/) · 2026-02
- He explains LLMs with analogies from computing and from human memory.
  > "GPT is a funny kind of programmable text computer." — [x/2023-04.md](https://x.com/karpathy/status/1644183721405464576) · 2023-04
  > "TLDR looking at LLMs as chatbots is the same as looking at early computers as calculators." — [x/2023-09.md](https://x.com/karpathy/status/1707437820045062561) · 2023-09
  > "But I do think that anything that's in the weights, it's a hazy recollection of what you read a year ago. Anything that you give it as a context at test time is directly in the working memory." — [youtube-appearances/unknown_lXUZvyajciY_andrej-karpathy-we-re-summoning-ghosts.md](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lXUZvyajciY&t=1167s) · 2025-10
- He coins short phrases that carry a whole idea.
  > "this network then dreams internet documents" — [youtube/2024-10-09_zjkBMFhNj_g_1hr-talk-intro-to-large-language.md](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zjkBMFhNj_g&t=559s) · 2023-11
  > "hi I'm Chach PT I am a one tab zip file my knowledge comes from the internet which I read in its entirety about six months ago and I only remember vaguely" — [youtube/2025-10-09_EWvNQjAaOHw_how-i-use-llms.md](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWvNQjAaOHw&t=742s) · 2025-02
  > "So what you end up with is this kind of a gradient superighway as I like to call it" — [youtube-appearances/unknown_u6aEYuemt0M_deep-learning-for-computer-vision-andrej.md](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u6aEYuemt0M&t=2841s) · 2016-09
- He has framed attention as communication for years, across lectures, talks and posts.
  > "attention is a communication mechanism you can really think about it as a communication mechanism where you have a number of nodes in a directed graph" — [youtube/2023-10-09_kCc8FmEb1nY_let-s-build-gpt-from-scratch.md](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kCc8FmEb1nY&t=4301s) · 2023-01
  > "Basically, to me, attention is kind of like the communication phase of the transformer" — [youtube-appearances/unknown_XfpMkf4rD6E_stanford-cs25-v2-i-introduction-to.md](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XfpMkf4rD6E&t=1367s) · 2023-01
  > "Attention is a token communication mechanism." — [blog/karpathy.github.io/2026__02__12__microgpt.md](https://karpathy.github.io/2026/02/12/microgpt/) · 2026-02
- Training an LLM is like taking it through school.
  > "We have to take the LLMs to school." — [x/2025-01.md](https://x.com/karpathy/status/1885026028428681698) · 2025-01
  > "just like you went to school to become um really good at something we want to take large language models through school" — [youtube/2025-10-09_7xTGNNLPyMI_deep-dive-into-llms-like-chatgpt.md](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7xTGNNLPyMI&t=7876s) · 2025-02
- He works through one concrete example instead of talking in the abstract, often from his own life.

## How it shows up

- One-line summaries compress a mechanism.
  > "Policy Gradients: Run a policy for a while. See what actions led to high rewards. Increase their probability." — [blog/karpathy.github.io/2016__05__31__rl.md](https://karpathy.github.io/2016/05/31/rl/) · 2016-05
  > "If training vanilla neural nets is optimization over functions, training recurrent nets is optimization over programs." — [blog/karpathy.github.io/2015__05__21__rnn-effectiveness.md](https://karpathy.github.io/2015/05/21/rnn-effectiveness/) · 2015-05
- For laypeople he uses one plain picture of training.
  > "In summary, the whole training process resembles showing a child many images of things" — [blog/karpathy.github.io/2015__10__25__selfie.md](https://karpathy.github.io/2015/10/25/selfie/) · 2015-10
  > "here's a cute dog and you probably may not know the breed of this dog but the correct answer is that this is a Japanese spaniel" — [youtube-appearances/unknown_Ucp0TTmvqOE_tesla-autonomy-day.md](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ucp0TTmvqOE&t=7093s) · 2019-04
- He borrows from films and daily life.
  > "In both of these movies, the protagonists, their weights are fixed and their context windows gets wiped every single morning" — [youtube-appearances/unknown_LCEmiRjPEtQ_andrej-karpathy-software-is-changing-again.md](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LCEmiRjPEtQ&t=1043s) · 2025-06
  > "The reality of building web apps in 2025 is that it's a bit like assembling IKEA furniture." — [x/2025-03.md](https://x.com/karpathy/status/1905051558783418370) · 2025-03
  > "A human body is like an iPhone with a battery pack that can grow nearly indefinitely" — [blog/karpathy.github.io/2020__06__11__biohacking-lite.md](https://karpathy.github.io/2020/06/11/biohacking-lite/) · 2020-06
- He ends lessons on punchlines that stick.
  > "So, if you try to find a new cool algorithm, look for oneline diffs that set something to zero." — [youtube-appearances/unknown_u6aEYuemt0M_deep-learning-for-computer-vision-andrej.md](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u6aEYuemt0M&t=2225s) · 2016-09
  > "we have these metallic objects traveling incredibly quickly and with very high kinetic energy and we are putting meat in the control loop" — [youtube-appearances/unknown_g6bOwQdCJrc_cvpr-21-wad-keynote-andrej-karpathy.md](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=g6bOwQdCJrc&t=27s) · 2021-06
- He shows the picture in his own head.
  > "the cartoon diagram that I have in my mind when I'm speaking to a model looks something like this" — [youtube/2025-10-09_EWvNQjAaOHw_how-i-use-llms.md](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWvNQjAaOHw&t=419s) · 2025-02
- He values a model that keeps the core dynamics but is easier to think with.
  > "The insights I like the most are the ones that point out mental models that distill a situation to something that preserves most of its core dynamics, but is easier to think about." — [blog/karpathy.medium.com/cs183c-assignment-3-649181b07fa0.md](https://karpathy.medium.com/cs183c-assignment-3-649181b07fa0) · 2015-11

## Tensions and evolution

- He uses human framings freely (tokens that "talk", models that go to school) and also warns where the analogy breaks.
  > "it helps to have multiple communication channels because obviously these tokens have a lot to talk about" — [youtube/2023-10-09_kCc8FmEb1nY_let-s-build-gpt-from-scratch.md](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kCc8FmEb1nY&t=5042s) · 2023-01
  > "I would caution you to uh not um kind of think of it too much like neurons because these are extremely simple neurons compared to the neurons you would find in your brain" — [youtube/2025-10-09_7xTGNNLPyMI_deep-dive-into-llms-like-chatgpt.md](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7xTGNNLPyMI&t=1484s) · 2025-02
- The anthropomorphic style goes back to how he was drawn into the field.
  > "And he kept using these anthropomorphic descriptions for what was going on in the algorithm. And I found that analogy fascinating." — [youtube-appearances/unknown_PlLmdTcsAWU_season-1-ep-1-andrej-karpathy.md](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PlLmdTcsAWU&t=268s) · 2021-09
