# Deep work and side projects

He protects long, uninterrupted stretches where he can get obsessed with one problem, and he keeps building small tools and projects for himself, publishing many of them.
  > "I feel like I need a span of a few days where I can really get in on that problem and I don't wanna be interrupted and I'm going to just be completely obsessed with that problem." — [youtube-appearances/unknown_cdiD-9MMpb0_andrej-karpathy-tesla-ai-self-driving.md](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cdiD-9MMpb0&t=8001s) · 2022-10

## Evidence

- He loads a problem into his head and keeps it there, even off the desk.
  > "you need to build some momentum on the problem without too much distraction and you need to load your ram, your working memory with that problem." — [youtube-appearances/unknown_cdiD-9MMpb0_andrej-karpathy-tesla-ai-self-driving.md](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cdiD-9MMpb0&t=7966s) · 2022-10
  > "And then you need to be obsessed with it when you're taking shower, when you're falling asleep, you need to be obsessed with the problem" — [youtube-appearances/unknown_cdiD-9MMpb0_andrej-karpathy-tesla-ai-self-driving.md](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cdiD-9MMpb0&t=7980s) · 2022-10
- Getting into a problem has a large fixed cost, so short slots don't work.
  > "there's always a huge fixed cost to approaching any problem." — [youtube-appearances/unknown_cdiD-9MMpb0_andrej-karpathy-tesla-ai-self-driving.md](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cdiD-9MMpb0&t=8024s) · 2022-10
- Even the chance of an interruption hurts his work.
  > "Not sure if there is a name for (I think no) the feeling of a deep discomfort when the probability of an interruption is > 0 while trying to work. It’s a kind of fear." — [x/2022-11.md](https://x.com/karpathy/status/1590604672557252609) · 2022-11
  > "Notifications. Masquerading as tiny and helpful but in reality psychologically invasive and damaging to the brain - interrupting complex thought, forcing (expensive, taxing) context switch, spiking dopamine, making thought reactive instead of proactive." — [x/2020-08.md](https://x.com/karpathy/status/1292239731707666432) · 2020-08
- llm.c was written in night sessions on a vacation, which he recommends.
  > "how most of llm.c was built at 1am-7am in a water villa porch in Maldives and why this is the recommended way to develop software" — [x/2024-10.md](https://x.com/karpathy/status/1841536804073439268) · 2024-10
- He runs teams so that deep, serial work is not split up, and he skipped recurring 1on1s.
  > "A good manager will understand the computational geometry of the project at hand and know when to delegate parts of it on the CPU or on the GPU." — [x/2024-04.md](https://x.com/karpathy/status/1780684098773876941) · 2024-04
  > "The most common stumbling point here is trying to parallelize something that was supposed to run on the CPU." — [x/2024-04.md](https://x.com/karpathy/status/1780684098773876941) · 2024-04
  > "I had ~30 direct reports and didn't do 1on1s (as a scheduled, regular activity) at Tesla and imo it was great." — [x/2024-05.md](https://x.com/karpathy/status/1796556328078619103) · 2024-05
- He builds his own tools instead of waiting for features.
  > "A normal person would request features and wait, but with the dark arts of extension hacking we can do much better." — [blog/karpathy.github.io/2013__11__23__chrome-extension-programming.md](https://karpathy.github.io/2013/11/23/chrome-extension-programming/) · 2013-11
  > "I hacked together a much nicer way to see #NIPS2012 papers last night" — [x/2012-11.md](https://x.com/karpathy/status/272425431880331265) · 2012-11
- He publishes projects to keep himself motivated and raise his own bar.
  > "your brain is a reward machine and it wants rewards and you need to give it rewards" — [youtube-appearances/unknown_tsTeEkzO9xc_andrej-karpathy-s-keynote-winner-pitches.md](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tsTeEkzO9xc&t=1200s) · 2024-07
  > "putting them up is actually also like a really good Jedi mind trick." — [youtube-appearances/unknown_tsTeEkzO9xc_andrej-karpathy-s-keynote-winner-pitches.md](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tsTeEkzO9xc&t=1251s) · 2024-07
- Small projects are fragile but some snowball, so he doesn't dismiss them.
  > "it's very easy to dismiss these small snowballs because they're so fragile right these projects are so fragile in the beginning" — [youtube-appearances/unknown_tsTeEkzO9xc_andrej-karpathy-s-keynote-winner-pitches.md](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tsTeEkzO9xc&t=896s) · 2024-07
  > "A lot of this is just exploration; you're tinkering and so actually these three projects didn't really go anywhere for me" — [youtube-appearances/unknown_tsTeEkzO9xc_andrej-karpathy-s-keynote-winner-pitches.md](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tsTeEkzO9xc&t=731s) · 2024-07

## How it shows up

- Many of his repos are single-purpose tools for his own life.
  > "How productive were you today? How much code have you written? Where did your time go?" — [github/ulogme/README.md](https://github.com/karpathy/ulogme/blob/416163e9c2399cc0f2f271630ccc6e15fe63a9fd/README.md) · 2014-05
  > "I leave this little web app always open at the corner of my screen." — [github/calorie/README.md](https://github.com/karpathy/calorie/blob/c030afbe9f38e7d0464d2a5d8a1871106dae4acd/README.md) · 2024-05
- arxiv-sanity came from his own frustration and runs on very little of his time.
  > "I felt continuously frustrated by the inefficiency of finding papers in the literature and I released and maintain the site in hopes that it can be useful to others" — [blog/karpathy.github.io/2016__09__07__phd.md](https://karpathy.github.io/2016/09/07/phd/) · 2016-09
  > "arxiv-sanity involves one person working 2 hours once a month (me)" — [blog/karpathy.medium.com/iclr-2017-vs-arxiv-sanity-d1488ac5c131.md](https://karpathy.medium.com/iclr-2017-vs-arxiv-sanity-d1488ac5c131) · 2017-03
- He thinks many personal tools should not be products at all.
  > "There will never be (and shouldn't be) a specific app on the app store for this kind of thing." — [x/2026-02.md](https://x.com/karpathy/status/2024583544157458452) · 2026-02
- To choose what to work on, he looks at what has given him energy over years, not this week.
  > "You can like low-pass filter yourself in terms of what has consistently been true for you." — [youtube-appearances/unknown_cdiD-9MMpb0_andrej-karpathy-tesla-ai-self-driving.md](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cdiD-9MMpb0&t=11774s) · 2022-10
- For research he picked his thesis problem by thinking several steps ahead, with a fallback if it failed.
  > "even if I were not successful I’d at least get lots of practice with optimizing interesting deep nets that I could reapply elsewhere" — [blog/karpathy.github.io/2016__09__07__phd.md](https://karpathy.github.io/2016/09/07/phd/) · 2016-09
- A paper gets one core contribution.
  > "Before you start writing anything it’s important to identify the single core contribution that your paper makes to the field." — [blog/karpathy.github.io/2016__09__07__phd.md](https://karpathy.github.io/2016/09/07/phd/) · 2016-09
- At conferences he goes for the people.
  > "Instead of attending a talk I encourage you to view the hallway as one of the main events that doesn’t appear on the schedule." — [blog/karpathy.github.io/2016__09__07__phd.md](https://karpathy.github.io/2016/09/07/phd/) · 2016-09
- He took a two-week break from the internet and noticed how stirred up his mind had been.
  > "~2 weeks is enough to settle into a lot more zen state." — [x/2024-04.md](https://x.com/karpathy/status/1776269310631235806) · 2024-04

## Tensions and evolution

- At Tesla the job drifted into management and away from code, which is part of why he left.
  > "In the beginning, when I joined Tesla, I was writing code and then I was writing less and less code, and I was reading code and then I was reading less and less code." — [youtube-appearances/unknown_cdiD-9MMpb0_andrej-karpathy-tesla-ai-self-driving.md](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cdiD-9MMpb0&t=9331s) · 2022-10
  > "And it's kind of like a corporate executive role. And I can do it. I think I'm okay at it. But it's not like fundamentally what I enjoy." — [youtube-appearances/unknown_cdiD-9MMpb0_andrej-karpathy-tesla-ai-self-driving.md](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cdiD-9MMpb0&t=6302s) · 2022-10
- No 1on1s at Tesla worked; trying them again at OpenAI did not.
  > "I went back to try 1on1s again at OAI and regret it." — [x/2024-05.md](https://x.com/karpathy/status/1796556328078619103) · 2024-05
- Obsession has a cost he measures in his own sleep data.
  > "my last week was rough because I was obsessed with a technical problem and couldn't sleep well" — [blog/karpathy.bearblog.dev/finding-the-best-sleep-tracker.md](https://karpathy.bearblog.dev/finding-the-best-sleep-tracker/) · 2025-03
- Side projects went from evening hacks to agent-built apps. Since December 2025 his side-projects folder has filled up.
  > "99% of this repo was vibe coded in a few hours with Opus 4.5." — [github/hn-time-capsule/README.md](https://github.com/karpathy/hn-time-capsule/blob/ca02ebb4de5e953bf1629689abd1f82b454b6905/README.md) · 2025-12
  > "it was just that realization that really uh had me um go down the whole rabbit hole of just, you know, infinity side project." — [youtube-appearances/unknown_96jN2OCOfLs_andrej-karpathy-from-vibe-coding-to.md](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=96jN2OCOfLs&t=128s) · 2026-04
  > "my side projects folder is like extremely full with lots of random things and uh just I've been coding all the time." — [youtube-appearances/unknown_96jN2OCOfLs_andrej-karpathy-from-vibe-coding-to.md](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=96jN2OCOfLs&t=135s) · 2026-04
